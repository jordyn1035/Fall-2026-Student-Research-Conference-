# How does audio affect sleep quality and duration?

For my DATA 211 data visualization project, I tracked my own sleep for 21 days to see whether playing audio throughout the night affected how long I slept and how well I felt I slept. I tested four conditions: no audio, audiobooks/ASMR, calming music, and white noise. Each condition was used for five nights, and one day was missed because of a power outage. Each night I recorded hours slept, a 1–10 sleep quality rating, and the disturbances counted by a sleep tracking app.

I analyzed the data in R, made graphs detailing the data, and used separate one-way ANOVA tests to compare sleep duration and sleep quality across the four conditions. Nights with audio tended to have higher quality ratings and fewer disturbances, but neither ANOVA found a statistically significant difference.

This repository contains the data, the R Markdown file, the knitted report, and the final poster.
